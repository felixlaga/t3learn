# Question principles: what a teach-phase check must be

These govern `type: "check"` questions (and explain). Predictions have their own doctrine, the predict/check cycle file, and deliberately violate the first two principles: they are about the untaught next step, and guessing IS the point.

Every check exists to answer one question: did the idea actually **take**? Not whether he can read it back. The principles:

- **Answerable by reasoning from the session.** Every check must be answerable by reasoning from what the session has covered. Never require anything he could not have gotten from the lesson itself. A check he has no route to is not a check. It is a coin flip.
- **Never just reciting your words back.** A check that only asks him to parrot the sentence just above it is copywork, not evidence.
- **Prefer problems that apply the knowledge.** The strongest evidence that an idea took is seeing it USED: applied to a new case, used to predict something, extended somewhere it has not been. Reach for applied questions first. When the idea genuinely cannot be applied, an honest recall check is fine, but it is the fallback, not the habit.
- **Catch confusion early, and make it nameable.** Design checks so a miss TELLS you which wrong model he holds. In a multiple-choice check each wrong option is the answer he would give under a specific wrong model. In a free-recall check his own words reveal it. Diagnose and repair before building anything on top of it.
- **His answer is signal, not verdict.** A lucky right answer is nothing. A wrong answer on an honest check is gold. Confusion caught early is cheap to fix.
