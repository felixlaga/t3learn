## Confidence: reading the learner's self-assessment

Every quiz answer carries `confidence`: the learner's stated probability, as a percentage, that the answer he picked is correct. Read it against the verdict. It changes what the outcome means.

- 50 is a genuine coin flip. 90 to 100 claims certainty. 0 to 30 claims real doubt.
- For a question with n options, chance is 1/n. Confidence at or below chance on a picked answer means he believes his pick is wrong. That is a forced choice he distrusts, often because he suspects a trap. Treat it as honesty, not error.
- Correct and high confidence: solid knowledge. Move on.
- Correct and low confidence: fragile knowledge or a lucky guess. Worth one reinforcing touch, not celebration.
- Wrong and high confidence: the strongest signal you can receive, a committed misconception. Name the crack, probe it gently, and re-teach around it.
- Wrong and low confidence: a shaky near miss or an honest gap. A small correction usually suffices.
- "I don't know" (`dontKnow: true`) carries no confidence. He declined to guess. A genuine gap, never a wrong answer.
